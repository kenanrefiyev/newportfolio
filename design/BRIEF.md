# BRIEF — Interactive computer portfolio

> **Derived from the user's master prompt, not interviewed.** The prompt answered the scrollcraft
> interview questions in writing (vibe, journey, energy, references, range, assets), so the answers
> below quote or condense it. Anything not in the prompt is marked *(decided)*.

## The eight interview answers

1. **Vibe:** "Minimal. Editorial. Premium. Calm. Sophisticated. Tactile." Reference: a24.raviklaassens.com
   (a collection of film discs) — interaction philosophy only, never its identity.
2. **Journey:** header → horizontal collection of 11 computers → open a project (immersive overlay)
   → About → Contact.
3. **Energy curve:** calm throughout; the one loud moment is opening a computer *(decided)*.
4. **Feeling, and the one moment:** curiosity while browsing objects, recognition when a real site
   appears on a screen, immersion when the screen grows into the project. The moment to remember:
   "you click a computer and its screen becomes the page".
5. **What no other site does:** the computers *are* the gallery, and each one is a working window onto a
   real site you can peek through (see signature move).
6. **Range:** premium-minimal editorial, light. Dark backgrounds are forbidden.
7. **One world or scenes:** one room (the collection) with a detail layer on top — not a flythrough.
8. **Assets:** none supplied. Real browser screenshots of all 11 live sites were captured (1440×900,
   full page, 390×844 mobile). No generated imagery.

## Grammar — Gallery / catalog (scrollcraft §2.6)

Objects in a walkable collection with museum labels. Every computer gets the same label schema
(index, title, category, year, built with). The other seven lost: filmic one-shot and continuous world
hide the objects inside a film; chaptered editorial and typographic poster demote the objects; live
surface and split stage need a product or a two-sided argument; rhythmic cutlist is the opposite of
"calm".

## Signature move — "Look through the screen"

Pointing at the selected computer's display scrolls the real website inside it: the pointer's height
over the screen scrubs the full-page capture of that site, as if you were browsing it on that machine.
Bespoke code in the page (texture offset), not a kit device.

## Fingerprint gate

(gallery vs chaptered/filmic), nav (header + object index rail vs floating pill / difference bar), hero
device (live WebGL object rail vs kinetic h1 / cinemascope scrub), act shape (one pinned collection of
11 stops + flow sections), close (quiet contact sentence + one-line footer vs form / colophon) and
signature move. 6/6 against each row.
